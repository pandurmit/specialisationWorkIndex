Brief Synopsis:

Boop Garden is a browser-based instrument designed for children aged roughly one to four. Each of the eight shapes plays one note of a pentatonic scale, so anything a child taps, slides across or bashes out on a keyboard sounds musical.

Every sound is synthesised live with the Web Audio API. Three picture buttons switch between bell, bubble and trumpet "sound worlds", and each shape opens its mouth to sing, bounces and sends out a floating note, so a child can see exactly what they caused.

Design Scenario:

Musical Toy for Toddlers

Prompt:

Design a way for young children with minimal language skills to explore music.

Context:

Browser based musical instruments offer users the ability to dynamically perform music and explore complex audio design. While these interactions allow for a depth of control, they can be intimidating for many adult users, let alone you children whose language skills are still developing.

How It Responds

  • No reading needed. Every control is a picture. The only words live behind a press-and-hold "grown-up corner".
  • No wrong notes. A pentatonic scale means random tapping always sounds pleasant, so exploration is rewarded, not corrected.
  • Music you can see. Rainbow order, size and height all map to pitch: bigger and lower is deeper, smaller and higher is higher.
  • Instant cause and effect. Each tap gets a sound, a singing face, a bounce and a floating music note, so children see what they caused.
  • Safe by design. Volume is capped and limited, and nothing on screen can navigate a child away.
